Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory

The sky belongs to everyone.

For more than 130 years, SAO has pushed the frontiers of astronomy — from the first measurements of our Sun to humanity's first image of a black hole. Your support keeps that work, and free public access to it, moving forward.

Ways to support

Be part of what we discover next

Every gift to SAO supports the same mission from a different direction. Choose the path that fits you.

Membership

Join the Smithsonian National Astronomy Council

SNAC members hear about discoveries before the public, straight from the astronomers making them — with virtual science forums, an annual Science Summit, and invitation-only travel to eclipses, rocket launches, and telescope sites. Membership starts at $1,500.

Explore SNAC membership

Leadership & legacy

Make a lasting impact

Leadership gifts become something specific in the world: an instrument, a named fellowship, an endowed fund, or a program that runs for years. Planned gifts carry that support beyond a lifetime.

Explore leadership giving

Organizations

Partner with us

The Corporate Partnership Program connects organizations to active research — with briefings from scientists, facility visits, technical exchanges, and recognition across Smithsonian platforms. Six levels from $5,000 to $250,000+.

Get started

Donate

Give today

Unrestricted gifts of any size go to work immediately, wherever the need is sharpest — the pilot study that earns a grant, the repair that keeps an instrument on the sky, the education program with no federal funding at all.

Where it goes

What your support makes possible

  • Instruments

    Building the tools

    Telescopes, detectors, and scientific instruments — including SAO's work for the Giant Magellan Telescope.

  • Facilities

    Keeping the sky open

    Observatories and research facilities in Cambridge, Arizona, Hawaiʻi, and at international sites.

  • People

    The next generation

    Graduate students, postdoctoral researchers, and early-career scientists doing the work that comes next.

  • Education

    Free and equitable access

    Science education programs for schools and educators, and free public access to SAO's science data.
